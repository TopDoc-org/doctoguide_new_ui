How {{ appName }} works

  1. Describe your symptoms in your own words — English, हिन्दी, Hinglish, or another language.
  2. Answer a few follow-up questions, the kind a clinician would ask before pointing you anywhere.
  3. Understand possible explanations in plain language, without a wall of worst-case search results.
  4. Understand how urgent it might be — whether this needs attention now, soon, or routinely.
  5. Decide what to do next, with the right speciality named and a summary you can carry to the consultation.

When you should seek medical care

Do not use a website — this one included — to decide whether an emergency is an emergency. Get help immediately for chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, drooping face or sudden trouble speaking, a seizure, confusion or unresponsiveness, heavy bleeding that won't stop, a severe allergic reaction, or thoughts of harming yourself. {{ emergencySentence }}

Anything sudden, severe, or rapidly worsening needs medical attention regardless of what any tool says. See medical safety for the full list, or emergency numbers by country.

{{ appName }} is not a replacement for a doctor

{{ appName }} cannot examine you, order tests, diagnose a condition, prescribe or adjust medication, or treat anything. It has no access to your history beyond what you type, and like any AI it can state something incorrect in confident-sounding language. A reassuring answer is not clearance.

Use it to arrive at a consultation better prepared — not instead of one. If you are choosing between reading this and seeing a doctor, see the doctor.
